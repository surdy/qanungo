# skill-finder

The write-posture half of qanungo's **skill & agent finder** (qanungo#13). `qanungo flows` computes the *finding* — repeated requests pooled across the whole archive, and the two- and three-step sequences they fall into, each quoted once (scrubbed) and cited by transcript `source_hash`; this skill reads those findings with the user and, where they agree one is worth tooling, drafts the skill or agent. qanungo stays read-only and LLM-free; the authoring happens here, in the harness, under the user's own permission prompts.

It is also the engine that would promote the other `contrib/` skills (`/standup`, `/coach`, `/cost-review`, `/ask`, `instructions-editor`) — but each of those still ships hand-reviewed, not auto-written.

## Steps

1. **Run the command.** `qanungo flows` — no positional argument, and **no default window**: absent `--last`, it reads all of the archive's history, which is what "what do I keep doing" means. Add `--last 4w` only when the user deliberately narrows (`h`/`d`/`w` units — no `m` by design). It folds *transcripts*, not summaries, so a cold run downloads the archive; say so before starting one, and let the footer report what it cost. Both sections are cut best-first under a line saying how many findings were held back; when either says anything is not shown, re-run with `--clusters` / `--flows` raised high enough that nothing is. The held-back findings are the *weakest*, which is the same weight class step 3 triages, and the sibling lesson from `instructions-editor` (qanungo#16) is that a reader who cannot raise the cut is reading a truncated input rather than a shorter one.
2. **Read the document for what it is.** Output: a scope line (sessions read, user messages, how many the harness wrote rather than the user, how many were comparable), a preamble stating what the tool cannot see, the thresholds that produced everything below, then **Repeated requests** — each `**N occurrences across M sessions in K repositories**` with its per-repository tally, one scrubbed blockquote, and a citation per occurrence (cut at eight) — then **Multi-step flows**, each a numbered list of step excerpts with one citation per occurrence (cut at eight) carrying `events a → b → c`, then **Repositories read**, **Gaps**, and the instrumentation footer.
3. **Triage before drafting anything, and do it with the user.** This is the step the lane exists to be fed into, and the top of the list is where it earns its keep. Sort every finding you intend to carry forward into one of four buckets, say which and why, and never draft from one the user has not agreed is real:
   - **Machinery.** The document's own preamble says the harness-noise list is a floor rather than a proof, and the pooled lens makes that worse rather than better: injected prose in an uncertified shape is byte-identical wherever it appears, so it clusters with itself in every repository at once and can honestly top the list. Skill bodies, MCP server instruction blocks, and injected repository instruction files are the observed shapes. A cluster whose blockquote is plainly something *the harness* put on the user surface is machinery, not a habit — say so and drop it.
   - **A workflow worth tooling.** A sequence of requests the user makes deliberately, in the same order, for the same kind of outcome. This is the only bucket that becomes a draft.
   - **One campaign.** A flow that recurred twice inside a week of work on one thing is a project, not a habit. The lane has no notion of a campaign and cannot tell those apart, so the user has to: ask whether they expect to do it again.
   - **Noise the thresholds let through.** A question they simply like asking, a paste, a smoke test. Real, repeated, and not a workflow.
4. **Read the instances before you generalize.** The blockquote is *one* occurrence's wording, clipped, and the clip is where drafts go wrong: the first real `instructions-editor` run completed a truncated quote with an invented word, and only redeeming the citation recovered what the archive actually said. So for any finding you are about to draft from, open the instances — the footer's `GET …/artifacts?original_sha256=<hash>` line, or `session-recall` with a hash in hand — and read the real steps at the ordinals the citation names. `qanungo ask --verbatim` is worth trying first and will often *fail* here, and knowing why matters: its search is summary-gated, so a request whose words never reached a summary does not rank. **Citation redemption is the backstop, not the search.** A step you cannot trace to the archive does not go in the draft.
5. **Decide skill, agent, or neither — out loud.** A repeatable *procedure* the user invokes → a **skill** (`SKILL.md`). A delegated, context-isolating *role* → a **subagent** (`.claude/agents/<name>.md`). A one-off, a campaign, or something the user already has tooling for → **neither**; say so and stop. Two findings that are the same workflow at different lengths — a three-step flow and its own leading two-step run both appear, by design — are *one* candidate, not two.
6. **Draft it, and show it before writing it.** Generalize from the instances: parameterize what varied across them, keep what was the same each time, and word the steps from the user's own repeated wording rather than from your paraphrase. Show the complete file and wait for approval. On approval, write it — `.claude/skills/<name>/SKILL.md` or `.claude/agents/<name>.md` — under the harness's normal permission prompts. New tooling files are this skill's whole write surface: never the archive, never qanungo's cache, never the user's source.
7. **Note provenance in the drafted file.** A line naming what it was promoted from: the occurrence count, the session count, the repositories, and at least one `source_hash`. A step you cannot trace to a finding is your idea rather than a finding — you may still propose it, but say so in those words and keep it out of the traced set.
8. **Carry the coverage.** **Repositories read** lists every repository the reach covered, and there is deliberately no "not examined" list under this lane — nothing was held back, which is worth saying rather than leaving to be inferred. A non-empty **Gaps** section is archived sessions that contributed nothing at all. Say these out loud rather than letting the answer imply the archive had nothing to show.

## What this skill must not claim

- **No outcome claim.** The lane reads *requests*. It does not know whether a flow worked, what it produced, how long it took, or whether the user was happy with it — none of that is in the fold at any resolution. "This flow works well for you" is a sentence no reading of these findings supports.
- **No causality.** A sequence in a transcript is an ordering. "You asked for B *because* A" is not something the document says or could say, and a drafted skill must not encode a causal story the archive did not record.
- **No worth claim of your own.** Whether a repetition deserves to be a skill depends on things the fold cannot see: how much of it was actually the same, whether the varying part was the interesting part, whether the user intends to do it again. Present the repetition and the judgement separately, and let the user make the second one.
- **No before/after claim.** Nothing in the archive marks when a skill was created, so no run can show that drafting one reduced the repetition. Re-running in a few weeks shows a *later* window, which is worth doing and is not a measurement; re-running over a shorter window changes what is counted, not what improved.
- **No reconstruction behind a marker.** See below.

## Redaction boundary (qanungo#8)

`flows` is the CLI's **third verbatim surface**, after `ask --verbatim` and `doctor`, and its excerpts are **already redacted, default ON** — `[REDACTED:<pattern>]` markers are load-bearing, and the footer names the pattern revision and counts what fired. This skill writes *files*, so the rule is as strict here as in `instructions-editor`: **never reconstruct what a marker hides into a drafted skill or agent file.** If a repeated request was "deploy with the token `[REDACTED:github-token]`", the drafted step names *which* credential and *where it lives*, never the credential itself — and a step that cannot be written without the hidden value is a step that does not belong in a file at all. Never ask the archive, the transcript, or the user to un-redact one so it can be pasted. Never pass `--no-redact` on your own initiative; if the user asks for it, note that off is a deliberate choice for their own local reading, that the footer confesses it in bold by design, and that its output must not be copied into a file either. The same applies to anything you read by redeeming a citation in step 4: the raw transcript is unredacted, and what you learned from it still goes into a file scrubbed.

## Boundaries

- **Read-only to the archive; write-only to new skill and agent files.** Nothing here writes to munshi, to Patwari, or to qanungo's cache, and nothing here edits source code or instruction files — proposing a `CLAUDE.md` edit is `instructions-editor`'s posture, under its own permission prompts.
- **Clusters here deliberately cross repositories**, which is the opposite of `doctor`'s rule and for a stated reason: an instruction file belongs to a repository, a workflow does not. A finding that turned up in one repository only is still a finding; the per-repository tally is what tells you which kind you are looking at, and a candidate that recurs in one repository alone may belong in that repository's `.claude/skills/` rather than in `~/.claude/skills/`. Ask; do not decide it for the user.
- **Sessions with no repository are in the pool**, unlike in `doctor`, and they show up in a tally as their own labelled bucket. A finding carried mostly by them is not a finding about a mystery repository — it is work done outside a checkout.
- **A flow's steps are *clusters*, not quotations.** The excerpt under a step is that cluster's representative wording — the fullest of its occurrences — and not any single instance of the flow. That is exactly why step 4 exists.
- **Adjacency is among clustered messages.** Intervening conversation does not break a flow apart, so the ordinals in one citation will have gaps between them. That is the design and not a defect; do not read the gap as missing evidence.
- **"Across N sessions" already means N sittings.** A resumed session replays what came before it, and the fold merges those into one conversation before counting.
- **The thresholds are arbitrary-until-measured constants**, stated in the document itself: phrase length, the overlap two messages need, the floor under a comparable message, the conversations a cluster and a flow each have to span, and the two- and three-step ceiling on a flow. A finding is what those settings found; the absence of one is not proof that nothing was repeated, and the empty result is phrased in exactly those terms.
- **munshi#77's tool signals are a named deferral, not a missing dependency.** The lane mines what a person typed; enriching a step with what ran after it gets pulled when a consumer needs it. Do not tell the user a flow is incomplete because tool sequences are not in it — tell them what the steps are.
- The instrumentation footer is for coverage and cost questions; drop it from a polished answer, and keep the citations.
- Related lanes: `instructions-editor` acts on `qanungo doctor`'s per-repository clusters, `/coach` reads the practice scores, `/ask` searches history, and `session-recall` opens a transcript once a session identity is in hand.
